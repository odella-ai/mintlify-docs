title: "How access control works"
description: "Understand capabilities, team roles, resource access lists, and the difference between using an AI Employee and authorizing its work."
---

Odella separates **who may use an AI Employee**, **who may change its settings**, and **what the worker may do**. Configure all three; instructions such as “only help Finance” are not a substitute for access controls.

## The access-control vocabulary

| Term                  | Meaning                                                                       | Example                                                |
| --------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Capability**        | Permission to perform a particular operation.                                 | View employee, Chat with employee, or Request work.    |
| **Role**              | A named set of capabilities assigned to people.                               | Support colleague.                                     |
| **Organization role** | A role whose assignment applies across the organization rather than one team. | An organization administration responsibility.         |
| **Team role**         | A role held by a person within a particular team.                             | Support colleague in Customer operations, not Finance. |
| **Primary access**    | The resource's main audience, optionally requiring a qualifying role.         | Customer operations members with Support colleague.    |
| **Additional access** | Explicit resource-level grants beyond the primary audience, where offered.    | A named reviewer who needs access to a Document Store. |
| **Editing area**      | A category of worker settings that an authorized editor may change.           | Working instructions, but not Sending & approvals.     |

A resource's audience and explicit grants form its **access control list (ACL)**. Roles describe permitted operations; resource rules determine where those permissions apply. Access is evaluated for the requested operation, not merely whether a button is visible.

<Note>
  An AI Employee's job title or responsibilities are not an organization
  permission role. Assign permission roles to people, then select the
  appropriate audience and role requirement on the worker.
</Note>

## Example: a team-limited worker

Suppose the primary access rule is **Customer operations** with **Support colleague**:

- A Customer operations member holding that role in that team can use the worker according to the role's capabilities.
- A member of another team does not qualify merely because they hold a role with the same name.
- Team membership alone does not satisfy a specific required-role rule.
- Viewing, chatting, requesting work, and viewing activity are separate capabilities. Do not grant activity access simply because someone needs to chat.
- The accountable manager and people with broad employee-administration access retain applicable access. A team restriction is not a way to exclude those administrators.

Review other grants and higher-privilege roles before claiming that a worker is accessible “only” to a particular group.

## Access does not imply editing

In **Settings → Governance**, choose who may edit and which areas they may change. A colleague might be allowed to update **Working instructions** without being allowed to change **Sending & approvals** or **Who can contact the Employee**.

Worker-specific configuration permissions are managed through Governance, not by adding those configuration capabilities to a general organization role. An editor must also have access to the worker; selecting someone as an editor does not itself add them to its audience.

The manager relationship is separate again. Read [Manager responsibilities](/workforce/manager-responsibilities) before assigning or transferring it.

## Permission to ask is not permission to act

A person authorized to request work has not thereby authorized every external action needed to finish it.

For example, a colleague can ask a worker to update a customer record, but the work still depends on:

1. The worker having the necessary connected tool and resource assignments.
2. The applicable user or account connection being available.
3. The provider account having permission to update that record.
4. The connected-tool policy allowing the operation or receiving the required approval.
5. Any separate outbound-recipient policy and workflow review steps.

An approval does not supply missing resource access or a missing provider connection. A connection does not override worker governance.

## Troubleshoot an unexpected result

| Symptom                                       | Check                                                                                                |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A colleague cannot find a worker              | Active organization, membership, primary team, required role, and View employee capability.          |
| They can view but cannot chat or request work | Chat with employee and Request work capabilities in the applicable scope.                            |
| An editor cannot change settings              | Worker access, selected editing policy, permitted areas, and any release-managed restrictions.       |
| Someone outside the team still has access     | Manager assignment, broad administration capabilities, and explicit resource grants where available. |
| A connected action fails                      | Worker tool assignment, connection owner, provider scopes, policy decision, and approval status.     |
| A role is missing from the audience selector  | Role type must match the audience scope and include View employee.                                   |

Test with ordinary members, not only administrators. For a complete setup and a positive/negative test matrix, follow [Create a team-restricted AI Employee](/platform/secure-employee-tutorial).
